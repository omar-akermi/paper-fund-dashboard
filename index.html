What the agents have learned

While the market is closed, the Researcher replays the last 20 sessions through this exact pipeline and records how every possible trade would have ended. The Learner tests rule changes on the older 70% of those trades and adopts one only if it also holds up on the newest 30% it never saw. This guards against "learning" from luck.

Strategy lab: what else could have been traded

After every session the Lab replays it bar by bar for about 120 liquid $2–$50 stocks under 12 different strategies, each in a long version (buy, profit when it rises) and a short version (sell first, profit when it falls; the same rule on the chart turned upside down), each scored with 5 exit rules (target 1R, 2R or 3R, hold to the close, or a trailing stop). The live fund trades only one of them (the 15-minute opening-range breakout, long) and never shorts; short results are what-if analysis only and ignore borrow fees. This shows how the others would have done on the same days. Dollar figures assume the live limits: $ risked per trade, at most trades a day. Costs (0.05% slippage each way) are included.

Breakout strategy: shadow book (10–11 Nov 2026)

Since 2026-11-11 the breakout trades the fund's money again (the user's choice), so this book is closed and kept as a record. On 2026-11-10 the opening-range breakout stopped trading the fund's money: a year of 5-minute data (8,214 trades under the fund's own rules) showed it losing 0.08R a trade while random entries under the same rules made +0.04R. It still runs every step of the pipeline (scan, timing, odds, news, sizing, stops) on a separate paper book of the same size, so its live record keeps growing. Nothing here touches the fund.

Self-improvement: the R&D agent

After each new trading session (never twice on the same data) the R&D agent runs a few pre-registered experiments. Claude designs up to 15 hypotheses from the research, each with a reason to expect an edge, and each is tested once on every archived session of 5-minute data for ~120 stocks (Yahoo serves 60 days; the fund keeps each finished session, so the history grows by a day each trading day). Since 2026-11-14 every test is compared with a market-day baseline: random stocks from the same list on the same days, entered at random times, with the same side, stop and exit. That baseline carries what the market gave everybody on those days and the cost of trading, so only a rule that picks better stocks or better moments beats it. (Before, the comparison was random entries in the same stock, which could only measure entry timing and was skewed by the signal itself; it is still shown as the timing edge.) To pass, a strategy must average at least +0.05R a trade, beat the market-day baseline by 0.05R or more with a chance of luck below 5% divided by the number of tests that session, hold up across days (t of 2 or more on daily averages), and be positive in both halves of the period. Rules can also use opening volume: how heavily a stock traded in its first 5 minutes compared with its usual. A pass is re-run with 8 nearby settings: at least 6 must stay positive. It then goes to shadow, where it is tracked on new sessions it was never tested on. Only a long strategy that keeps working there moves to live trading, and anything whose new-session results turn bad is retired. (Before this, a brute-force search tried 75,000 strategies and found only luck.)

Library: what the agents found online

Every day the Librarian agent searches arXiv and Semantic Scholar for research on intraday and short-term stock patterns, and Claude turns the new papers into ideas R&D can test, each with a skeptic's note. Newsfeed

The Newsroom agent reads CNBC, MarketWatch, Nasdaq, Google News, PR Newswire, the Federal Reserve, SEC filings (8-K) and Yahoo Finance every 3 minutes on trading days. Each headline is tagged with the companies it names, its topic and a simple tone (green = positive words, red = negative). Before the open, at midday and after the close, Claude turns them into the brief below. A buy is blocked when the company itself had bad news in the last 18 hours (an offering, downgrade, lawsuit, halt, bankruptcy and the like).

Multi-year pattern study

Every evening the Researcher reloads 4 years of daily and about 3 years of hourly prices for the same ~120 stocks and checks about 40 patterns: gaps, streaks, oversold and overbought readings, candlestick signals, day of the week, the first trading hour, and more. Each is scored as a day trade (in at the open or signal, out at the close, after 0.1% costs). The bars show the average move for a buyer: right means buying paid, left means shorting would have. A pattern only counts as a candidate if it is clearly away from zero (t of 2 or more, measured across days because stocks move together), points the same way in the older and newer half of its days, and beats costs. With 40 tests, one or two will pass by luck, so a candidate is something to test in the Strategy Lab, not a rule to trade.

Glossary

Opening range (ORB)
The high and low of the first 15 minutes of trading (9:30 to 9:45 ET). The strategy buys when a 5-minute bar closes above that high: a breakout.
Stop
The price where the position is sold to cap the loss. Set before buying, at the middle or the low of the opening range.
Target
The price where profit is taken, set as a multiple of the risk (1R, 1.5R or 2R above the entry).
R
One unit of risk: the distance from entry to stop. Results in R compare trades of any size or price.
Oracle / Time Machine
Replays the rest of the day 10,000 times from the stock's own recent price moves, plus every past day the same setup fired, and estimates the average R. The fund skips trades it rates poorly.
Pattern bias
A score from recent daily candlestick patterns (hammer, engulfing, evening star and others), positive when bullish and negative when bearish.
Volume ×
The breakout bar's volume compared with the average bar in the opening range. Above 1× means stronger participation.
Flat by close
Every position is sold by 15:55 ET. This is a day-trading fund: nothing is held overnight.
Paper trading
Simulated: real live prices, no real money and no broker. Fills include a small slippage allowance.
