What the agents have learned

While the market is closed, the Researcher replays the last 20 sessions through this exact pipeline and records how every possible trade would have ended. The Learner tests rule changes on the older 70% of those trades and adopts one only if it also holds up on the newest 30% it never saw. This guards against "learning" from luck.

Strategy lab: what else could have been traded

After every session the Lab replays it bar by bar for about 120 liquid $2–$50 stocks under 12 different strategies, each in a long version (buy, profit when it rises) and a short version (sell first, profit when it falls; the same rule on the chart turned upside down), each scored with 5 exit rules (target 1R, 2R or 3R, hold to the close, or a trailing stop). The live fund trades only one of them (the 15-minute opening-range breakout, long) and never shorts; short results are what-if analysis only and ignore borrow fees. This shows how the others would have done on the same days. Dollar figures assume the live limits: $ risked per trade, at most trades a day. Costs (0.05% slippage each way) are included.

Self-improvement: the R&D agent

After each trading day (and every 6 hours at weekends) the R&D agent runs a few pre-registered experiments. Claude designs up to 15 hypotheses from the research, each with a reason to expect an edge, and each is tested once on the last ~60 sessions of 5-minute data for ~120 stocks. Every test is compared with a random-entry baseline: the same stocks, days, side, stop and exit, but entered at random times. That baseline carries the day's market move and the cost of trading, so only a signal that adds something beats it. To pass, a strategy must average at least +0.05R a trade, beat random entries by 0.05R or more, do so with a chance of luck below 5% divided by the number of tests that session, and be positive in both halves of the period. A pass is re-run with 8 nearby settings: at least 6 must stay positive. It then goes to shadow, where it is tracked on new sessions it was never tested on. Only a long strategy that keeps working there moves to live trading, and anything whose new-session results turn bad is retired. (Before this, a brute-force search tried 75,000 strategies and found only luck.)

Newsfeed

The Newsroom agent reads CNBC, MarketWatch, Nasdaq, Google News, PR Newswire, the Federal Reserve, SEC filings (8-K) and Yahoo Finance every 3 minutes on trading days. Each headline is tagged with the companies it names, its topic and a simple tone (green = positive words, red = negative). Before the open, at midday and after the close, Claude turns them into the brief below. A buy is blocked when the company itself had bad news in the last 18 hours (an offering, downgrade, lawsuit, halt, bankruptcy and the like).

One-year pattern study

Every evening the Researcher reloads a full year of daily and hourly prices for the same ~120 stocks and checks about 40 patterns: gaps, streaks, oversold and overbought readings, candlestick signals, day of the week, the first trading hour, and more. Each is scored as a day trade (in at the open or signal, out at the close, after 0.1% costs). The bars show the average move for a buyer: right means buying paid, left means shorting would have. A pattern only counts as a candidate if it is clearly away from zero (t of 2 or more, measured across days because stocks move together), points the same way in both halves of the year, and beats costs. With 40 tests, one or two will pass by luck, so a candidate is something to test in the Strategy Lab, not a rule to trade.

Glossary

Opening range (ORB)
The high and low of the first 15 minutes of trading (9:30 to 9:45 ET). The strategy buys when a 5-minute bar closes above that high: a breakout.
Stop
The price where the position is sold to cap the loss. Set before buying, at the middle or the low of the opening range.
Target
The price where profit is taken, set as a multiple of the risk (1R, 1.5R or 2R above the entry).
R
One unit of risk: the distance from entry to stop. Results in R compare trades of any size or price.
Oracle / Time Machine
Replays the rest of the day 10,000 times from the stock's own recent price moves, plus every past day the same setup fired, and estimates the average R. The fund skips trades it rates poorly.
Pattern bias
A score from recent daily candlestick patterns (hammer, engulfing, evening star and others), positive when bullish and negative when bearish.
Volume ×
The breakout bar's volume compared with the average bar in the opening range. Above 1× means stronger participation.
Flat by close
Every position is sold by 15:55 ET. This is a day-trading fund: nothing is held overnight.
Paper trading
Simulated: real live prices, no real money and no broker. Fills include a small slippage allowance.
